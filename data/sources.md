# Assumption sources

Where each number in the model came from, and where it's a rough estimate rather than a sourced figure.

## Cask purchase price — ₹4,00,000 (~£3,000-5,000)

New-fill hogsheads from UK brokers typically run £1,800-£12,000 depending on distillery and cask type, with £2,300-£5,000 the more common range for a standard 200-500L cask. Used £3,000-5,000 as the base for a 500L hogshead, converted at ~₹107/GBP.
Sources: Mark Littler Ltd ("How Much Is a Cask of Whisky?"), Master of Malt whisky guide.

## Angel's share — 1.5-2.5%/year (Scotland)

Standard climate-driven evaporation rate for casks matured in Scottish bonded warehouses. India/tropical climate figure (~10-12%/year) sourced separately and used only as a comparative reference point in earlier drafts, not in the final model, since the asset itself must mature in Scotland to legally qualify as Scotch.

## Storage, insurance, regauge costs

Storage ~£95/cask/year, insurance ~£350/year (covers up to £100,000 across multiple locations), regauge (ABV/volume check) ~£66 every 3-5 years. Simplified to a flat ₹150/year in the model — a rough approximation, not a precise conversion.
Source: Mark Littler Ltd, cask ownership cost breakdown.

## Exit commission (10%) and illiquidity haircut (5%)

Estimated, not directly sourced from a single broker's published fee schedule. Real costs vary by broker and by how quickly a seller needs the transaction done. Flagged as an assumption, not a fact, in the memo.

## ABV cliff — 40% minimum

Legal requirement for a spirit to be sold as Scotch whisky. Regulatory fact, not an estimate.

## Demand-decline scenario range (-10% to +3% aging premium)

Grounded in real 2024-2025 Scotch secondary market data, not an arbitrary pessimistic guess:
- Single malt Scotch auction transaction value fell 53% in the four months to January 2025 vs. the same period the prior year (Noble & Co Whisky Intelligence Report, via Decanter and The Spirits Business).
- The Knight Frank/Rare Whisky 101 whisky index dropped 9% in 2024; a separate Mark Littler 999 Index dropped 11% the same year.
- Trading volumes fell 26% and total market value fell 40% between April and July 2025 (Scotch Whisky Investments market report), before showing signs of stabilising.

The exact bounds (-10% to +3%) are a defensible interpretation of this data, not a scientifically derived range — a different reasonable analyst could pick a milder or steeper range. Worth sensitivity-testing further.

## Tax and regulatory treatment (India-specific)

- Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS): Indian residents can remit up to $250,000/year for overseas asset purchases (RBI/FEMA).
- Tax Collected at Source (TCS): 20% on LRS remittances above ₹7 lakh, recoverable against final income tax liability, not a final cost.
- UK Capital Gains Tax: whisky casks are classed as a "wasting asset" (predictable life under 50 years) and are CGT-exempt in the UK, conditional on remaining in bonded storage.
- This UK exemption does not extend to Indian tax residents — gains are taxable in India under standard capital gains rules regardless of UK treatment.
- The asset must be disclosed under Schedule FA (Foreign Assets) in the investor's Indian income tax return; non-disclosure falls under the Black Money Act.

## What isn't priced into the model

Indian capital gains tax on exit, and GBP/INR exchange rate movement over the holding period. Both apply to a real Indian investor and would change the realised, after-tax, after-FX return. Left as an open limitation rather than modelled with an assumed rate, since both are genuinely uncertain over a 10-15 year horizon.
